# Building Hierarchical Topic Clusters to Silo Your Local Site

A pile of service pages is not a site structure. Google's AI layer reads your whole website to judge how deeply and coherently you cover your subject — and a flat collection of disconnected pages reads as shallow, no matter how many keywords each page carries. A *hierarchical* structure, where broad topics branch cleanly into specific ones and the pages link to each other in a deliberate pattern, is what signals genuine topical authority. This lesson covers how to design that structure for a local business and wire it together.

This is the structural counterpart to GBP–website alignment. Alignment makes your profile and site say the same thing; topical structure makes your site say it *thoroughly*.

## Step 1: Define the niche and the focus area

Start with two inputs: the business niche, and the single main area of interest you want the site to be known for. For a pest control company, the niche is pest control and the focus area is *types of pest control*. Being this specific up front keeps the entire structure anchored — every page that follows should ladder back up to that focus area.

## Step 2: Generate the hierarchy with an LLM

Hand those two inputs to a capable language model (a purpose-built custom GPT works well, but any strong model will do) and ask it to define a hierarchical structure of topic clusters around the main topic, organized under the focus area. You're looking for three levels:

- **Primary category** — the broad subject (pest control).
- **Subtopics** — the major branches (residential, commercial, integrated pest management, eco-friendly, chemical, biological, fumigation).
- **Sub-subtopics** — the specific angles under each branch. Under biological pest control, for example: predatory insects, plant-based controls, advantages and limitations.

The first pass gives you the skeleton. Then explicitly ask the model to expand it — request further subdivisions and additional branches. This second pass is where coverage gets comprehensive: preventative measures, professional-services breakdowns, commercial sub-niches like office buildings and food service. Push until the map feels exhaustive rather than stopping at the first tidy answer.

## Step 3: Turn the hierarchy into a page architecture

The topic map converts directly into two kinds of pages:

- **Pillar pages.** Comprehensive, long-form pages covering a broad topic in depth. Your primary category and each major subtopic each earn a pillar: pest control, types of pest control, residential pest control, commercial pest control, integrated pest management, eco-friendly pest control.
- **Cluster content.** Focused articles on the specific sub-subtopics — one page each for ants, cockroaches, termites, sealing entry points, and so on.

Every cluster article belongs to exactly one pillar. That ownership is what makes it a silo rather than a tangle.

## Step 4: Wire the internal links deliberately

Structure only pays off if the links reinforce it. Five rules:

1. **Pillar to pillar.** Each pillar page links out to the other pillar pages, so the broad topics form a connected top layer.
2. **Pillar to cluster, and cluster back to pillar.** A pillar links down to every cluster article it owns; each cluster article links back up to its pillar. This two-way link is the core of the silo.
3. **Contextual links between related articles.** Within the body of an article, link to genuinely related pages — the "common household pests" article links to ants, cockroaches, and termites.
4. **Hierarchical direction.** Links should flow general → specific and specific → general, following the tree. Residential pest control links down to common household pests; common household pests links back up to residential. Avoid links that jump sideways across unrelated silos.
5. **Breadcrumbs and upkeep.** Use breadcrumb navigation so both users and crawlers can see the hierarchy at a glance, and audit the link structure on a regular schedule as pages are added or retired.

You can have the same model draft the full interlinking plan for you — for each pillar, which pages it should link to, and for each cluster, its contextual links — once the page list is settled. Treat its output as a first draft to sanity-check, not a finished sitemap.

## Why this matters for local

A local business rarely needs hundreds of pages, which makes the temptation to publish a flat handful of service pages strong. But depth and structure are exactly what distinguish a site Google's AI reads as authoritative from one it reads as thin. A well-siloed topic hierarchy, combined with a Google Business Profile that aligns to it, gives the algorithm a single coherent, high-confidence picture of what you do and how thoroughly you do it — and that picture is what holds map pack positions.
