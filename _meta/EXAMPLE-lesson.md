# Choosing Your Google Business Profile Primary Category

Your primary category is the single most influential on-profile signal for which searches you appear in. Google treats it as a declaration of what your business *is*, not just what it *does*. Pick the wrong one and you'll rank well for the wrong terms — or for nothing at all.

## The competitor audit method

The reliable way to pick a primary category is to reverse-engineer it from what's already ranking:

1. List the top three searches you want to rank for. Write them exactly as a customer would type them.
2. For each search, look at the three-pack (map pack) and note the primary category used by each business ranking in it. Tools like Local Falcon or browser extensions like GMBspy will surface this.
3. If the same primary category appears across all three searches, that's your category. If different categories appear, pick the one closest to the *most commercially valuable* search — the query that actually drives leads.

This outperforms guessing from Google's category picker because it's grounded in what Google is already rewarding, not what you think should work.

## Secondary categories widen your surface area

Primary category carries most of the ranking weight, but secondary categories let you show up for adjacent searches. You can add up to nine. A couple of rules:

- Only add a secondary category if you genuinely offer that service. Irrelevant categories don't help — Google suppresses your profile for searches where your category doesn't match the user's intent.
- Don't stuff. More categories isn't better. Three or four relevant ones beat nine loosely-related ones.

## The most common mistake

Picking the "prestigious" category instead of the accurate one. A consultant who helps businesses market themselves might want to be "Consulting Agency" because it sounds bigger. But if the searches driving revenue are `marketing agency near me`, Google will rank "Marketing Agency" profiles for that query, not "Consulting Agency" ones.

Pick the category that matches the searches, not the category that matches your pitch deck.
